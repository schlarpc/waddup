# Can we *prove* a purpose-built hash-binding proof system is secure? Literature map (2024–2026)

Target statement: "I know F with SHA-256(F)=H1 and BLAKE3(F)=H2" (plus SHA-1/SHA-512/MD5 variants), proved by a hand-written circuit + a small Rust verifier run by every P2P client. Question: what could formal verification (Lean etc.) actually deliver, at what cost, with what residual trust.

## 1. Layer-by-layer survey

### 1a. Circuit-level correctness

**Clean (zkSecurity / EF Verified-zkEVM)** — the most relevant artifact. An embedded Lean 4 DSL whose `FormalCircuit` objects carry reusable soundness ("any witness satisfying constraints+lookups ⟹ spec holds") and completeness ("honest witness satisfies constraints") proofs, composable across subcircuits. Gadget directory today: `Addition32, Addition8, And, BLAKE3, ByteDecomposition, Keccak, Not, Or, Rotation32, Rotation64, SHA256, Xor` plus bits/boolean/lookup helpers ([repo](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/clean), [gadgets](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/clean/tree/main/Clean/Gadgets)). The SHA-256 folder is a bit-level *compression function* (message schedule + 64 rounds + feed-forward), "proven correct with respect to the Lean SHA-256 specification in `Clean/Specs/SHA256.lean`", no lookup tables so it is R1CS-compatible, needs field `p > 2^33` ([README](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/clean/tree/main/Clean/Gadgets/SHA256)). Not covered: padding, multi-block iteration, arbitrary-length messages. BLAKE3 has `BLAKE3G, Round, ApplyRounds, Permute, Compress, FinalizeChunk, FinalStateUpdate` — again the primitive, not the chunk tree ([dir](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/clean/tree/main/Clean/Gadgets/BLAKE3)). zkSecurity's comparison post rates Clean and sp1-lean the most mature frameworks ("Level 2: useful components like hash functions or VM instructions") and notes most frameworks lack completeness proofs ([post](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/formal-verification-arithmetic-circuits/)). The Poseidon write-up shows the theorem shape and that the last `sorry` was BN254 primality, closed by a Pratt certificate in CompPoly ([post](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/poseidon-clean/)). Roadmap: "polish plonky3 backend, generate constraint evaluation Rust code" (in progress), Stwo/R1CS backends, LLZK import. New "Channels" primitive for multi-table systems is demonstrated on a toy Fibonacci VM; local-to-global completeness lift is "future work" ([post](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/clean-verified-zkvms/)). **I could not verify whether the SHA-256 and BLAKE3 gadget proofs are `sorry`-free** — check `#print axioms` before relying on them.

**sp1-lean (Succinct + Nethermind)** — proves, for each SP1 Hypercube RV64 chip, "if a row satisfies its constraints, it computes what the RISC-V Sail model specifies", against constraints mechanically extracted from Rust by `sp1-constraint-compiler` and expressed in Clean. Soundness for all chips is sorry-free; five completeness proofs are deferred; memory argument, LogUp, STARK protocol, recursion are explicitly out of scope ([repo](https://github.com/succinctlabs/sp1-lean), [Succinct blog](https://blog.succinct.xyz/nethermind-lean/)). The EF post-mortem on a JALR completeness bug is instructive: the Lean theorem carried an unproven hypothesis that *masked* the bug, which was found by compliance tests; "51 of 62 claimed opcodes have complete, correct proofs" ([EF blog](https://zkevm.ethereum.foundation/blog/sp1-fv)). Lesson for waddup: hypotheses in theorem statements are where completeness bugs hide.

**Picus / LLZK (Veridise)** — automated *determinism* (uniqueness) checking. Picus natively supports Circom, R1CS, gnark; LLZK (MLIR IR) lifts Circom, Halo2, Plonky3 (Noir in progress) into it ([LLZK](https://veridise.com/blog/veridise-announcements/announcing-llzk-a-unified-open-source-intermediate-representation-ir-for-zero-knowledge-languages/)). Applied to RISC Zero's Zirgen: Keccak accelerator determinism confirmed in <3 min over 45k constraints ([Veridise/RISC Zero](https://veridise.com/blog/audit-insights/risc-zeros-zk-vm-security-how-veridise-enabled-risc-zero-to-achieve-provable-continuous-zk-security/)); applied to SP1 (Plonky3): 11 core ops verified, manual I/O annotation, no modular decomposition in Plonky3 yet ([Veridise/SP1](https://veridise.com/blog/audit-insights/verifying-sp1-circuit-determinism-with-picus-a-collaboration-between-veridise-and-succinct/)). Picus proves *uniqueness only*, not correctness vs spec. No public report of Picus on a SHA-256 circuit specifically (not found; unverified).

**Halva (Nethermind, Halo2→Lean, 2025)** — extracted Scroll's Keccak-256 circuit and found an under-constrained `is_final` column letting a prover claim any Keccak output for 8-byte-aligned inputs; partial soundness proven (absorb/squeeze, padding for single-repetition) ([blog](https://www.nethermind.io/blog/formal-verification-of-halo2-circuits-in-lean)). Direct precedent that hash circuits harbour exactly the bug class FV catches.

**Others**: zkLean (Galois; R1CS/lookup/MLE combinators, Jolt extractor) ([repo](https://github.com/GaloisInc/zkLean)); Jolt-QED verifies Jolt bytecode expansions vs Sail for 51/58 programs, no hash precompiles ([eprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2157)); Nethermind's Zirgen-MLIR→Lean VC generator for RISC Zero ([blog](https://www.nethermind.io/blog/towards-formal-verification-of-the-first-risc-v-zkvm)); Lampe (Noir→Lean, active) ([repo](https://github.com/reilabs/lampe)); ProvenZK + gnark-lean-extractor used for World ID Semaphore MTB ([repo](https://github.com/reilabs/proven-zk)); CODA (Coq/Circom) and risc0-lean4 are dormant; Certora's EF grant covers Powdr autoprecompile equivalence, no hash specifics yet ([blog](https://www.certora.com/blog/securing-zkevm-optimizations)). StarkWare/Avigad's Lean proof that the S-two AIR encoding of Cairo is sound is the largest AIR-soundness proof to date ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04311), [repo](https://github.com/starkware-libs/formal-proofs)) — it covers the AIR, not the circle-STARK protocol or verifier binary.

### 1b. Protocol-level soundness

**ArkLib (EF Verified-zkEVM, Lean 4)** — Interactive Oracle Reductions as the compositional core; README lists sum-check, Spartan, Merkle trees, FRI + coding theory, STIR, WHIR, Binius as "actively being worked on"; ROADMAP marks FRI/STIR/WHIR and Binius "under active development" and **BCS & Fiat-Shamir "planned"** ([repo](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib), [roadmap](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/blob/main/ROADMAP.md), [Lean use-case page](https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/arklib/)). Depends on VCVio (oracle computations, ROM, forking lemma, FS for sigma protocols — not yet multi-round BCS) ([VCVio 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/899)) and CompPoly. Stated intent: verify functional equivalence of executable specs with Rust code extracted via hax/Aeneas — "aspirational rather than implemented". Least Authority's grant is only a Lean *blueprint* for STIR/WHIR completeness+soundness ([blog](https://leastauthority.com/blog/advancing-zkvms-with-formal-verification/)).

**Standalone results**: Lean formalization of "A Simplified Round-by-round Soundness Proof of FRI" — ~1.5 months, one formalizer (Y. Hirai) with Aristotle + Claude Code, one WHIR mutual-correlated-agreement lemma assumed, not yet merged into ArkLib ([zkSecurity](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/simple-rbr-fri/)). **Isabelle/STARK** (Marmsoler, Aug 2026): FRI + Merkle + Fiat-Shamir + AIR for a toy square-sequence, completeness = 1 and adaptive soundness ≤ 2^-137 under 2^20 oracle queries in a field-valued ROM; 487 theories; no ZK, no extraction, no link to any implementation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01965), [repo](https://github.com/dmarmsoler/isabelle-stark)). Sum-check soundness/completeness in Isabelle/AFP ([CSF'24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06093)). Bailey–Miller: Lean (3) soundness of Groth16 and five other linear-PCP SNARKs in an algebraic model, decision-procedure style, no implementation link ([USENIX'24](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/bailey)). EasyCrypt: Firsov/Unruh sigma-protocol/Fiat-Shamir/forking lemma ([2025/573](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/573)), KZG ([2025/1972](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1972)).

**Important 2025–26 development**: the up-to-capacity proximity-gap / mutual-correlated-agreement conjectures (BCIKS, WHIR, DEEP-FRI) were **disproven** ([eprint 2026/782](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/782), [arXiv 2604.09724](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09724)); soundness **up to the Johnson bound is unaffected**, and the SoK recommends Johnson-bound parameters with "modest" repricing ([SoK 2026/1367](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1367)). Any waddup verifier should set FRI parameters at the Johnson bound so the protocol claim rests on proven theorems.

Distinction to keep in mind: IOP-level statements (what ArkLib/FRI-RBR give) are information-theoretic; the deployed non-interactive proof additionally needs BCS/Fiat-Shamir in the ROM plus Merkle binding — the layer ArkLib has not built and where only Isabelle/STARK (toy) and VCVio (sigma protocols) have machine-checked results.

### 1c. Implementation-level correctness

**Aeneas/Charon** (Rust→pure Lean via ownership) is now the recommended Lean path even inside hax ([hax README](https://github.com/cryspen/hax)). Flagship: Microsoft SymCrypt — safety, panic-freedom and functional correctness of 16.7 KLOC Rust (SHA-3, ML-KEM; FrodoKEM/ML-DSA/HPKE experimental) with 237 KLOC of Lean, AI agents writing many proofs, kernel-checked ([arXiv 2609.15648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15648)). Directly ZK-relevant: the "Rust-to-Lean pipeline with AI provers" report extracted and (partly) proved Plonky3 FRI folding, Mersenne31/KoalaBear arithmetic, Horner evaluation, `compute_log_arity_for_round`, and RISC Zero Merkle inclusion against ArkLib/CompPoly specs; pain points were toolchain drift and extraction limits ([arXiv 2605.30106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30106)). HashCloak's Privacy-Cash effort shows a typical wall: ark-bn254 const-generic hierarchies could not be translated and were shimmed ([repo](https://github.com/hashcloak/Verified-Privacy-Cash)). Aeneas limits: safe Rust subset, no labelled multi-level break, no generics instantiated with `&mut` ([tracking issue](https://github.com/AeneasVerif/charon/issues/142)).

**Verified-verifier precedent**: Nethermind proved zkSync's on-chain Yul PLONK verifier is *honest* (bisimulation with the paper's verifier) in EasyCrypt in ~2.5 months including learning curve; PLONK soundness, Fiat-Shamir, completeness explicitly not covered ([blog](https://www.nethermind.io/blog/we-verified-the-verifier-a-first-for-zero-knowledge-proof-systems), [repo](https://github.com/NethermindEth/zksync-verification)). ZKProof's "Verified Verifiers" WG (Groth16, EasyCrypt + Lean) targeted a 12-month deliverable from May 2024; I found no published complete verified Groth16 verifier from it (unverified) ([page](https://zkproof.org/verifier/)). Verified hash code reusable in the verifier: libcrux-sha2 (HACL*-derived, hax/F*-verified, pre-release) ([crate](https://crates.io/crates/libcrux-sha2)); HACL* SHA-2 in C; AWS-LC SHA-384/512 via SAW/Cryptol ([repo](https://github.com/awslabs/aws-lc-verification)); Appel's Coq/VST OpenSSL SHA-256 ([paper](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/verif-sha.pdf)). Verus/Kani/Creusot: no ZK verifier precedents found; Kani is useful for cheap panic-freedom/bounded checks only.

**EF's own leanerVM** is worth watching: a Lean 4 executable-semantics + formalization of leanEthereum's hash-based (binary fields, BLAKE2s) VM built on ArkLib/VCVio/CompPoly/Clean; six opcode tables proven as Clean components; proof-system correctness "pending"; recursion "open research"; differential-tested against Rust ([repo](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/leanerVM)). It is the closest thing to "the whole stack in one prover" and shows where the frontier is.

### 1d. Specifications

Lean 4: SHA-256 spec inside Clean (`Clean/Specs/SHA256.lean`; KAT testing not documented); BLAKE3 pure Lean (`Blake3.Pure.hash`, one-shot unkeyed only, validated against BLAKE3 1.8.7 vectors, merged Sept 2026) ([PR](https://github.com/argumentcomputer/Blake3.lean/pull/50)); Keccak-256 (ProofBundle, and SymCrypt SHA-3 spec KAT-tested). SHA-1, MD5, SHA-512 in Lean: not found (ProofBundle SHA-512/256 and SHA-256 repos are self-described "SCAFFOLDED"). Cryptol has SHA-2 family; HACL*/F* has SHA-1, MD5, SHA-2 as spec-level definitions. Writing an executable Lean spec of each and testing against NIST/RFC vectors is days each.

### 1e. Toolchain trust (irreducible after everything above)

Lean kernel + Mathlib's three axioms (re-check with `lean4checker`); Charon/Aeneas translation semantics and `rustc`/LLVM; Clean's circuit semantics and its Plonky3 code generator (or the manual correspondence if you hand-port constraints); the random-oracle model for the transcript hash and collision resistance of the Merkle hash; proximity gaps at the Johnson bound (proven) — do **not** rely on capacity conjectures; grinding/RBR accounting for the concrete bit-security figure.

## 2. Complete verified verifiers: what exists

Nothing covers all three of {circuit sound/complete, protocol knowledge-sound in ROM, binary equals spec} for one production system. Closest partial stacks: Isabelle/STARK (protocol, toy AIR, no code); zkSync/EasyCrypt (code honesty only); sp1-lean + Clean (circuit only); S-two AIR (circuit/AIR encoding only); SymCrypt (code only, not ZK); leanerVM (in progress across all layers). Groth16 has the most protocol formalization (Bailey–Miller) but no verified verifier binary and a trusted setup.

## 3. Which proof system is most amenable today

1. **Small-field STARK, FRI, Plonky3-style AIR (recommended)**. Every relevant artifact points here: Clean targets Plonky3 AIRs and already has SHA-256/BLAKE3 gadgets; sp1-lean proves the Rust→Clean extraction workflow; LLZK/Picus lift Plonky3; ArkLib's active items are FRI/STIR/WHIR; the FRI RBR proof is in Lean; Isabelle/STARK gives a full-protocol template; the Rust-to-Lean pipeline already touched Plonky3's FRI fold and Mersenne31/KoalaBear. WHIR/STIR would reduce verifier work but their formalizations are earlier-stage (blueprints).
2. **GKR / sum-check**: sum-check is well formalized (Isabelle AFP; ArkLib single round sorry-free), but a hash-binding circuit over sum-check still needs a multilinear PCS (FRI/WHIR or Binius) and Clean has no GKR-shaped gadgets; more novel proof obligations, not fewer.
3. **Binius64**: no formal artifacts found beyond ArkLib's "binary tower fields and Binius" in progress; binary-tower arithmetic and its PCS add unformalized math. Least amenable today despite being a natural fit for SHA-256/BLAKE3 (XOR-heavy).
4. **Groth16**: best protocol-level formal coverage and small verifier, but pairings in Rust are a large verification burden (see Privacy-Cash's bn254 shim), the circuit would be R1CS (Clean's SHA-256 gadget is R1CS-compatible, so reuse is possible), and the trusted setup is a trust assumption a P2P tool cannot discharge with any theorem.

## 5. What a credible claim looks like, and recommendation

**Minimal credible "verified" claim (≈10–20 PM, 1–2 people, ~1 year):**
1. Executable Lean specs for SHA-256 and BLAKE3 (reuse), SHA-1/MD5/SHA-512 (write), all KAT-tested.
2. Clean circuits for full-message SHA-256 and BLAKE3 plus the shared-input binding, with sorry-free soundness *and* completeness theorems (`#print axioms` in CI); Picus/LLZK determinism pass on the actual Plonky3 AIR as a cross-check.
3. Verifier written in Aeneas-friendly Rust (no unsafe, simple generics, use a verified SHA-256 such as libcrux-sha2 as the Merkle/transcript hash), extracted with Charon/Aeneas, and proven equivalent to a Lean reference verifier — the zkSync-style "verifier honesty" theorem.
4. Protocol soundness *cited*, not proven: paper proofs + the Lean FRI-RBR formalization, parameters at the Johnson bound, concrete bit-security computed and documented.
Honest wording: "the circuit is machine-checked to compute exactly SHA-256/BLAKE3, the shipped verifier is machine-checked to implement the specified STARK verifier; STARK soundness relies on published proofs under the ROM and Johnson-bound proximity gaps."

**Maximal claim (+20–35 PM, gated on ArkLib):** replace step 4 with a machine-checked ROM knowledge-soundness theorem for the concrete AIR→FRI→Merkle→Fiat-Shamir pipeline in ArkLib (needs its BCS layer, still "planned"), and close the Clean-model-to-Rust-constraint-evaluator gap by verifying Clean's generated evaluation code or extracting the Rust constraints back into Lean (sp1-constraint-compiler approach).

**Recommendation.** Build the purpose-built system as a Plonky3-style small-field STARK with FRI at Johnson-bound parameters, author the hash circuits in Clean from day one (so the Lean proof is the source of truth and Rust constraints are generated or extracted, not hand-mirrored), and write the verifier in the Aeneas subset. Do steps 1–3 of the minimal claim now; treat the protocol-level theorem as a later upgrade that rides on ArkLib. This is the only choice where each layer has an existing Lean library and a public precedent within the last 18 months; Binius64 and GKR would each require pioneering formalization work, and Groth16 trades a verifiable statement for a trusted setup.
